Machine Claims

Every claim, with its receipt attached.

When a machine claims a result, the claim should arrive with its receipt: the exact artifact, its hash, and the record of how it was tested. TSCP is working software for building evidence chains that keep claims and their evidence attached.

We corrected ourselves in public

Our earliest headline benchmark claimed the AVX-512 kernels ran 9.15× faster than scalar code. A number that good deserved suspicion, so we rebuilt the measurement: 50 samples, dual runs, a frozen and documented protocol. The result came out at 1.27×. We published the correction ourselves, and both numbers stay visible on this page. That correction is the founding story of this project: evidence over flattery, including our own.

What TSCP is

Working evidence-chain software with a machine-checked verification core, built to answer one question: how does anyone check what a machine claims about its own work?

  • Evidence chains. Each claim is bound to its bytes. Artifacts, hashes, and test records stay attached to the claims they support.
  • A machine-checked core. The critical arithmetic (Montgomery reduction) is verified in Lean 4, with exact, file-scoped counts published below.
  • On-chain anchoring. Evidence digests are anchored on the Sepolia testnet, providing a public reference against which later record changes can be detected.
  • Public review packages. Reviewer-access software, benchmarks, and the evidence chain are public under Apache-2.0. Some additional development and proof sources remain private.
